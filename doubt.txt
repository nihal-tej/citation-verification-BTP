Your revised implementation plan primarily focuses on legal citation verification.

Its central idea is:
A citation may point to the correct judgment and paragraph, but that paragraph may represent a party's argument or factual account rather than the court's own reasoning.

For example:
- A legal proposition claims that a particular action is unlawful.
- A citation points to paragraph 25 of Case X.
- Paragraph 25 records the petitioner's argument that the action is unlawful.
- But the court subsequently rejects that argument.
Your existing verifier is designed to identify this mismatch.

It includes:
- Citation and pinpoint resolution.
- Rhetorical-role identification.
- Claim-type classification.
- Legal support/contradiction checking.
- Statutory applicability checking.
- PASS, REJECT, or ABSTAIN decisions.
This is a valid research problem. Your document also proposes a benchmark called InCite-IN to evaluate citation verification
